I build it, then I try to break it

My team MacHackers took 2nd in Toronto and 3rd at the CyberSci Regional Finals 2025. I write code like someone is going to attack it, because someone usually does.

I finished my Computer Science degree at McMaster in June 2026, with a minor in Statistics. My time there went two ways: building real software, and learning how to take it apart.

[build] I was the only developer on two apps for Environment and Climate Change Canada, from the first prototype to signed releases on Azure that scientists actually log into.

[defend] I work alerts end to end: Splunk, Windows event logs, packet captures, then a write-up of what really happened. CTFs keep me honest. It's a lot easier to catch an attack you've run yourself.

Slicc

Group bill splitting with real E2E chat

Capstone mobile app for splitting bills and picking restaurants, built by a 7-person team and shipped to Android.

MindVault

Study lockdown enforced by your own firewall

Desktop app that closes your distractions for 1 minute to 8 hours while a study desk and offline games stay open.
